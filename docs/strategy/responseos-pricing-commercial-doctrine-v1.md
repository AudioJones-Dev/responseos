This document defines the current commercial pricing doctrine for ResponseOS.

It establishes:

* product positioning,
* pricing architecture,
* offer tiers,
* implementation fees,
* managed-service fees,
* contract structure,
* provider-usage treatment,
* capacity boundaries,
* target customer profile,
* commercial qualification rules,

## 2. Canonical Product Positioning

Ratified

ResponseOS is:

Managed revenue-response infrastructure for founder-led service businesses.

ResponseOS is not primarily sold as:

* an AI receptionist,
* a chatbot,
* a voice agent,
* a telephony product,
* a CRM replacement,
* or commodity automation software.

Its commercial purpose is to improve how inbound revenue opportunities are:

* captured,
* qualified,
* routed,
* followed up,
* recorded,
* escalated,
* monitored,
* and continuously improved.

The commercial value comes from managed operational responsibility, not merely access to AI technology.

## 3. Canonical Commercial Architecture

Ratified

The canonical ResponseOS commercial model is:

Assessment + Implementation + Managed Operations + Transparent Provider Usage

The four layers are:

1. Assessment

Used where business leakage, workflow complexity, operating rules, integrations, or readiness are unclear.

2. Implementation

One-time professional services required to discover, design, configure, integrate, test, launch, and document the operating system.

3. Managed Operations

Recurring service covering monitoring, QA, optimization, integration health, reporting, governance, provider administration, and bounded operational improvements.

4. Provider Usage

Variable third-party costs such as:

* telephony,
* voice AI,
* model/API usage,
* SMS,
* transcription,
* email delivery where material,
* workflow execution,
* specialized storage,
* and other client-specific runtime costs.

Provider usage is not the primary source of ResponseOS service margin.

ResponseOS pricing is determined primarily by:

* number and complexity of integrations,
* CRM/FSM requirements,
* routing and escalation complexity,
* support obligations,
* monitoring requirements,
* governance requirements,
* reporting requirements,

ResponseOS is not priced primarily according to:

* AI minutes,
* token volume,
* number of agents,
* number of prompts,

Those variables may affect provider usage and operating capacity, but they do not define the core commercial value.

$1,500 one-time

Purpose

Used when the prospect’s:

* revenue leakage,
* intake process,
* CRM condition,
* system architecture,
* operational rules,
* workflow complexity,

Deliverables

The standard assessment should include, where applicable:

* current response-path analysis,

* workflow map,
* CRM/FSM findings,
* stack review,
* operational bottlenecks,
* prioritized ResponseOS use case,
* implementation scope,
* capacity recommendation,

Credit Policy

The standard $1,500 assessment may be credited 100% toward a qualifying Managed Core implementation when:

* the client proceeds within 30 days,
* the scope remains substantially consistent,
* and no extraordinary fieldwork or custom architecture was required.

A paid assessment is not mandatory for a simple, already-bounded pilot.

Status: Ratified / Sellable Now

Pricing

Implementation: $8,500
Managed service: $1,500/month
Provider usage: Separate
Minimum term: 90 days after go-live

Purpose

A tightly bounded implementation designed to validate one meaningful revenue-response workflow under supervised conditions.

Target Buyer

Typically:

* $1M–$3M+ annual revenue,
* founder-led service business,
* one location,
* meaningful inbound opportunity volume,
* urgent or high-value leads,
* one clear revenue leakage problem,
* and limited integration complexity.

Standard Capacity Envelope

The Pilot is designed around approximately:

* 1 legal business unit,
* 1 location,
* 1 primary revenue-critical workflow,
* 1 CRM or FSM,
* 1–2 primary inbound channels,
* 1–2 production integrations,
* bounded operating rules,
* bounded optimization,
* business-hours support,
* monthly operating review.

Included Managed Scope

May include:

* workflow design,
* intake logic,
* qualification logic,
* routing,
* escalation logic,
* CRM synchronization,
* booking or callback workflows,
* provider configuration,
* launch QA,
* operational reporting,
* monthly workflow health review,

* provider reconciliation,
* and documented operational handoff.

Explicit Exclusions

Unless separately scoped, the Pilot does not include:

* unlimited workflows,
* unlimited locations,
* unlimited integrations,
* unlimited custom development,
* unlimited client-requested changes,
* custom dashboards beyond agreed reporting,
* advanced SLA coverage,
* complex multi-role governance,
* enterprise security requirements,
* regulated-data architecture,
* causal revenue attribution,
* or 24/7 custom incident response.

Commercial Interpretation

The $8,500 + $1,500/month structure is appropriate only when the Pilot remains bounded.

It should not be used as a low-cost path into an implementation that functionally resembles Managed Core or Managed Operations.

## 7. Provider Usage Doctrine

Ratified

Provider usage is billed separately and transparently.

Preferred Model

Use client-owned provider accounts where operationally practical.

Examples:

* telephony,
* AI model providers,
* messaging,
* CRM,
* automation,
* voice providers,
* email platforms,
* storage,
* and specialized APIs.

AJ Digital may receive delegated administrative access.

Secondary Model

Where provider accounts must be operated by AJ Digital:

Pass provider usage through at cost unless a separate provider-administration fee has been expressly approved.

The current doctrine does not automatically apply a percentage markup to provider usage.

Provider Usage Examples

May include:

* Telnyx,
* Twilio,
* OpenAI,
* Retell,
* Vapi,
* Bland,
* Deepgram,
* transcription,
* SMS,
* phone numbers,
* carrier fees,
* provider-specific compliance fees,
* specialist APIs,
* and high-volume automation costs.

Reporting Rule

ResponseOS financial reporting should distinguish:

Service gross margin

from

Bookkeeping margin including reimbursed provider spend

Pass-through provider spend must not be used to make service margin appear stronger or weaker than it actually is.

## 8. Margin Doctrine

Ratified

The primary margin risk in ResponseOS is not AI inference cost.

It is human operational workload.

The business must monitor:

* implementation hours,
* QA workload,
* exception frequency,
* integration maintenance,
* provider administration,
* monthly support time,
* client communication,
* workflow-change requests,
* and incident response.

Pilot Economics

At $1,500/month, recurring delivery should normally remain within a narrow operational envelope.

Current working assumption:

Target: approximately 6–12 managed delivery hours per month.

This is an operational assumption and must be validated using live client data.

Core Economics

At $2,500–$3,500/month, ResponseOS must rely on:

* standardized monitoring,
* reusable workflows,
* reusable capability modules,
* automated reporting,
* systematic QA,
* documented change control,
* and vertical playbooks.

A Core client should not function as an open-ended custom-development engagement.

Operations Economics

Higher monthly fees exist partly to support:

* more active QA,
* more integrations,
* higher exception volume,
* greater governance,
* more frequent optimization,
* and stronger service obligations.

## 11. Revenue Attribution Doctrine

Ratified

ResponseOS must distinguish between:

Potential Revenue

Value of opportunities entering the system.

Not recovered revenue.

Estimated Influence

Modeled impact based on assumptions.

Not recovered revenue.

Verified Booking or Job

Observable booking, estimate, dispatch, or job event.

Not automatically recovered revenue.

Collected Revenue

Revenue recorded as collected in the system of record.

Not automatically attributable to ResponseOS.

Attributable Recovered Revenue

Collected revenue that can be reasonably demonstrated to be incremental and caused by ResponseOS under an agreed attribution methodology.

Only this final category should be described as attributable recovered revenue.

* persistent operating context,
* provenance,

* SOP retention,

## 25. Conditions That Would Justify Raising Prices

Pricing should be reconsidered upward if:

* standard Core implementations regularly exceed $20,000 equivalent labor,
* live customers demonstrate strong recovered gross profit,
* monthly support burden exceeds current assumptions,
* clients renew comfortably at higher retainers,
* multi-location deployments become common,
* SLA and monitoring obligations increase,
* or vertical-specific ResponseOS playbooks demonstrate high measurable value.

---

## 26. Conditions That Would Justify Lowering or Narrowing Prices

Pricing should be reconsidered downward or scope narrowed if:

* buyers consistently choose low-cost SaaS after understanding the scope,
* Pilot implementation regularly cannot justify $8,500,
* $1,500 Pilot accounts consistently exceed the target support envelope,
* the current product remains primarily a voice-agent implementation,
* market willingness to pay in the target vertical is materially lower,
* or deployment complexity cannot be standardized.
